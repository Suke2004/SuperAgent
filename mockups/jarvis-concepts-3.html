The screen is a window, not a page. Two physical planes: your words are carved into the near surface (dark, engraved, catching a highlight), JARVIS lives behind the glass — larger, softer, glowing, drifting a few pixels against your text as you tilt the phone. No bubbles, no bars, no cards. The only chrome is a hairline bezel and a caret sitting on the glass.
  • Parallax is driven by the gyro, so the depth is real, not decorative — and it makes "who said this" a spatial fact rather than a colour convention.
  • One turn at a time by design; history is a pinch-out gesture that pulls the glass stack apart.
  • Trade-off: the etched style is low contrast by nature. I'd ship it with a "flatten" accessibility toggle that raises contrast and stops the motion.
  • Build cost: medium — expo-sensors for tilt, layered text with shadows. Reduce-motion must disable the drift.

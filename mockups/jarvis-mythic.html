Jarvis — marks drawn from mythological symbols

Eight candidates, all in the Claude-referenced palette you approved: ivory #faf9f5, clay #c1603c, Georgia for the wordmark. Every mark is a single currentColor stroke drawing on a 64×64 grid, so one file serves the app icon, the tab bar and the in-message spinner.

The large glyph on each card is animating right now — that is the real proposed thinking state, not a still. Beside it is the size ramp (30px tab glyph, 18px inline), then the mark on three icon backgrounds, then the thinking row as it would appear in a message.

One thing you should decide with open eyes. The swastika (02) is a genuine and ancient auspicious symbol, and in an Indian context it reads exactly as you intend. Outside that context the identical shape is a banned Nazi emblem: it is illegal to display in Germany, Austria and several other countries, and both the Apple App Store and Google Play have removed apps carrying it as hate imagery, usually without letting you argue the etymology. If Jarvis is only ever for you, it costs you nothing. If it ships to a store, expect rejection or a takedown, and your app icon is the one asset you cannot contextualise. That is why 03 exists: the Nandyavarta pinwheel is the same four-fold rotational auspicious geometry with curved arms, so it keeps the meaning and loses the silhouette.

Formally, it is the best mark on this page. Four bent arms make a perfect rotational lock, it is unmistakable at 18px, it needs no fill, and rotating it slowly is a natural thinking state — the arms read as a pinwheel catching air. The dots in the pockets fire in sequence so motion is visible even when the rotation is too slow to see.
  • Rotate it clockwise, never anticlockwise. The clockwise form is the auspicious one; the reversed sauvastika carries the opposite association and also happens to match the Nazi tilt more closely.
  • Keep it upright, not on the 45° diagonal. The diagonal presentation is the one people misread instantly.
  • Store risk is real and not arguable — see the note at the top. Fine as a personal build; a liability if you publish.

What I would do

01 Sudarshana Chakra, with 03 Nandyavarta as the alternative if you want the swastika's exact symbolism without its exact shape. The chakra wins for one reason that is not aesthetic: it is the only mark whose meaning requires motion, so the thinking animation is not decoration bolted onto a logo — it is the logo behaving correctly. And a discus that is dispatched, strikes its target and returns is a description of your gateway.
  1. Skip 02 unless this stays private. It is the best-drawn shape here and I would use it in a heartbeat if the world were fair about it. It isn't, and the app icon is the one surface with no room for a caption.
  2. Pair the mark with Georgia "Jarvis" — the serif already carries the Claude reference, and the Devanagari subtitle in the lockups is available if you want the origin stated rather than implied.
  3. Build: one src/components/Glyph.tsx with state: idle | thinking | writing | error, react-native-svg plus Reanimated for the rotation, and useReducedMotion swapping the spin for a slow core fade. Then assets/icon.png, the adaptive-icon foreground and monochrome layers, the splash and the favicon all regenerate from this same 64×64 source.
  4. One dependency to add: react-native-svg is not in package.json. 01, 02 and 05 could be faked with rotated plain views; 03, 04, 06, 07 and 08 cannot.
